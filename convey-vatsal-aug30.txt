  ## Recommended Scientific Questions

  The paper should answer four explicit questions:

  1. Controlled benchmark: Under identical data splits and budgets, how do TabPFN generations, TabFM, AutoML and strong tree models compare in discrimination, probabilistic
     accuracy and computation?

  2. Within-cancer prediction: Can molecular features predict fixed-window overall-survival outcomes within individual cancer cohorts?
  3. Pooling: Does pooling cancers reveal a shared prognostic signal, or does it allow models to exploit cancer/source identity?
  4. Generalization: Does performance survive within-cancer stratification, cancer-held-out testing and independent-cohort validation?

  ## Proposed Hypotheses

  - H1: No single foundation model dominates every metric; TabFM, TabPFN and tuned tree models exhibit different discrimination, calibration and computational trade-offs.
  - H2: Per-cancer multiomics prediction is unstable because the datasets are high-dimensional and sample-limited.
  - H3: Randomly pooled cancer evaluation produces higher global AUC partly because molecular modality and missingness patterns identify cancer cohorts.
  - H4: Performance will decline under leave-one-cancer-out and external-cohort validation if the apparent signal is dominated by cohort shortcuts.
  - H5: Extreme-survival selection improves class separability but changes the target population and therefore cannot be interpreted as improvement on the original fixed-
    window task.

  H3 and H4 are currently hypotheses supported by diagnostic evidence, not yet final conclusions.

  Paragraph 3: Independent evaluation gap

  Explain that vendor or developer benchmarks do not automatically establish performance under identical preprocessing, data caps, hardware, model versions and probabilistic
  metrics. Introduce reproducibility, calibration and compute as separate dimensions.

  Paragraph 4: Biomedical stress test

  Introduce cancer multiomics as a difficult (p \gg n) setting with censoring, heterogeneous modalities, technical batch effects and cohort-specific molecular distributions.
  Batch-label confounding can severely inflate cross-validation estimates, as demonstrated in this genomic classification study.

  Paragraph 5: Study objective

  State that this study independently evaluates tabular foundation models and then stress-tests their apparent performance in five cancer cohorts using increasingly
  stringent validation.
